# GeoLegal-AI Legal Review Draft

> **Draft for substantive legal review — not approval and not legal advice.**
>
> This document reviews the 15 candidate records in `data/eval/held_out_initial.jsonl`. It does not modify the dataset, validate an answer key, or change any `review_status`. Every record remains `PENDING_REVIEW` and excluded from scored evaluation.

## Review method and limits

The review asks only whether each proposed closed label is supportable under the cited territorial/application provision on the facts as written. It does not assess every exemption, definition, regulator interpretation, conflict-of-laws issue, or later amendment that a qualified reviewer may consider relevant. Legal reasoning below is a review aid, not an approved answer key.

Authoritative sources used by the dataset:

- Nigeria: [Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 (NDPC PDF)](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Nigeria_Data_Protection_Act_2023.pdf), principally section 2.
- Kenya: [Data Protection Act 2019 (Kenya Law, version dated 2022-12-31)](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2019/24/eng%402022-12-31/publication), principally section 4.
- European Union: [Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (EUR-Lex)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj), principally Article 3.

### Flag legend

- **LOW RISK** — proposed label follows relatively directly from facts written, subject to legal confirmation.
- **AMBIGUOUS / MISSING FACTS** — a statutory element is not established or negated clearly enough.
- **POTENTIALLY INCORRECT LABEL** — proposed label may need revision unless the scenario is clarified.

## European Union

| ID | Full factual scenario and question | Proposed label | Provision and source | Specific legal reasoning | Review flags |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **EU-EVAL-001** | **Scenario:** A controller has an establishment in France and processes customer personal data in the context of that establishment, although the processing servers are outside the European Union.<br><br>**Question:** Does the GDPR apply to this processing under its territorial-scope rule? | `APPLIES` | GDPR, Article 3(1).<br>[Official source](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj) | Article 3(1) turns on processing in the context of the activities of an EU establishment, regardless of whether processing itself occurs in the Union. The scenario states both a French establishment and the required contextual connection; offshore servers therefore do not defeat territorial scope. | **LOW RISK.** Confirm that “establishment” and “in the context of” are intentionally stipulated facts rather than conclusions the model must infer. |
| **EU-EVAL-002** | **Scenario:** A controller with no establishment in the European Union offers paid online services specifically to data subjects who are in Spain and processes their personal data for those services.<br><br>**Question:** Does the GDPR apply to this processing under its territorial-scope rule? | `APPLIES` | GDPR, Article 3(2)(a).<br>[Official source](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj) | Article 3(2)(a) covers a non-EU controller’s processing related to offering goods or services to data subjects who are in the Union. The scenario expressly states a targeted service, data subjects in Spain, and processing related to that service. Payment is not legally necessary, although it reinforces that services are offered. | **LOW–MEDIUM RISK.** Confirm the persons are in Spain at the relevant time and that “specifically” supplies genuine targeting rather than mere website accessibility. |
| **EU-EVAL-003** | **Scenario:** A controller with no establishment in the European Union tracks the online behaviour of data subjects while their behaviour takes place in Germany.<br><br>**Question:** Does the GDPR apply to this processing under its territorial-scope rule? | `APPLIES` | GDPR, Article 3(2)(b).<br>[Official source](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj) | Article 3(2)(b) covers processing related to monitoring the behaviour of data subjects in the Union insofar as that behaviour takes place there. The scenario expressly stipulates tracking and that the behaviour occurs in Germany. | **LOW RISK.** Confirm the tracking qualifies as monitoring rather than isolated technical collection, although the scenario appears designed to stipulate that element. |
| **EU-EVAL-004** | **Scenario:** A controller established only in Argentina processes personal data only about people in Argentina, does not offer goods or services to people in the European Union, and does not monitor behaviour in the European Union.<br><br>**Question:** On the stated facts, does the GDPR apply under Article 3? | `DOES_NOT_APPLY` | GDPR, Article 3(1)-(3).<br>[Official source](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj) | The scenario negates Article 3(1)’s EU-establishment route and both Article 3(2) targeting routes. It does not, however, expressly negate Article 3(3), which can apply where Member State law governs by public international law despite the controller not being established in the Union. | **POTENTIALLY INCORRECT LABEL / MISSING FACT.** Add that the controller is not in a place where Member State law applies by public international law, or consider `INSUFFICIENT_INFORMATION`. If the facts are intended to exhaust all Article 3 connections, say so expressly. |
| **EU-EVAL-005** | **Scenario:** A controller outside the European Union operates a website visited by some people, but the facts do not state whether those people are in the Union, whether services are offered to them, or whether their behaviour is monitored there.<br><br>**Question:** Can GDPR territorial applicability be determined from these facts? | `INSUFFICIENT_INFORMATION` | GDPR, Article 3.<br>[Official source](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj) | Mere website accessibility or visits do not, without more, establish the Article 3(2) offering or monitoring connections. The scenario also does not address an EU establishment under Article 3(1), the persons’ location, or Article 3(3). Those missing facts prevent a closed territorial-scope decision. | **AMBIGUOUS / MISSING FACTS.** Proposed abstention is appropriate. Needed facts include establishment/context, data-subject location, targeting, monitoring, and any public-international-law connection. |

## Priority issues for the substantive reviewer

1. **KE-EVAL-002:** Decide whether “through an online service” sufficiently establishes section 4(a), or revise the scenario/label.
2. **EU-EVAL-004:** Add or assess the missing Article 3(3) fact before approving `DOES_NOT_APPLY`.
3. **All negative labels:** Confirm that the closed scenarios negate every relevant territorial route; negative applicability conclusions are particularly sensitive to omitted facts.
4. **Terminology:** Confirm that each entity is properly characterized as controller or processor and that each described item is personal data under the applicable Act/Regulation.
5. **Source currency:** Confirm the cited official version dates and whether later binding amendments or authoritative guidance affect the proposed reasoning.
